What this actually is

A random forest trained on molecules from the ESOL solubility dataset, held out by Bemis–Murcko scaffold rather than at random, so the test molecules were structurally novel. On that honest split it reaches R² and a mean absolute error of log units.

The interval is the spread of the 100 trees, and the domain check is the Tanimoto similarity to the nearest training molecule — below the threshold, the training set simply contains nothing like your input. Both are computed live: RDKit is compiled to WebAssembly and the forest is evaluated in JavaScript, so nothing is sent anywhere and there is no server.

Caveats worth stating. Solubility is only one property, measured under specific conditions; ESOL is small and skews toward drug-like organic molecules. Inorganics, salts and polymers are outside its scope by construction, which is exactly why the domain gauge exists. A confident-looking number with a red gauge is still a guess.
