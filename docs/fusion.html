Can it tell when it's wrong?

A Bayesian neural network that predicts how tightly a molecule binds a protein, using both the ligand's 2D graph and the 3D geometry of the binding site. It reports an uncertainty with every prediction. The question worth asking is whether that uncertainty means anything — so this page checks it against the errors it made.

What the model is

FusionAffinityNet has two encoders. A GINE network reads the ligand's 2D graph with its bond features; a SchNet-style network reads the 3D positions of ligand and pocket atoms through radial-basis distance filters. Their outputs are concatenated into a Bayesian head trained by Bayes-by-Backprop, which learns a distribution over weights rather than point values. Sampling that distribution gives epistemic uncertainty, which more data should reduce; a predicted log-variance gives aleatoric uncertainty, which it should not.

Trained on the PDBbind v2015 refined set, held out by Bemis–Murcko scaffold so test ligands are chemically novel rather than close analogues of the training set. That split is considerably harder than the usual core-set holdout, and the numbers above are lower because of it. Ideal calibration would be 68% and 95% coverage.

Read this cautiously. Affinity prediction benchmarks are known to overlap heavily with training data, which inflates published figures; the scaffold split here is an attempt to avoid that, not a guarantee. The pocket is everything within 6 Å of the ligand, so the model never sees the wider protein. And a single held-out split, however honest, is one sample.
